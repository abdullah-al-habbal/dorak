# Toptalla: validation plan and decision metrics

Routes: [Toptalla hub](index.md) · [competitor analysis](../index.md) · previous: [11 Privacy, compliance and risk](11-privacy-compliance-and-risk.md) · back to: [01 Executive summary](01-executive-summary.md)

**Scope of this file:** a 90-day practical plan to turn the public evidence into a decision, the metrics to track, what to request before a purchase or partnership, and the study's closing conclusion. It consumes the open questions raised in every other file of the study.

## Practical validation plan

| Period | What is executed | What changes the decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–14** | Meet 8–12 centers from the target segments and run a trial on one branch. Record a baseline for cancellations, onboarding time, cash-register close time and manual front-desk messages. Ask for a written offer and the privacy and export documents. | A mismatch on a necessary function, inability to export data, or ambiguity about settlement cost or encryption = do not roll out. |
| **Days 15–45** | Test 3–5 nearby centers, with booking from web, Android, iOS and WhatsApp, multi-service appointments and different branches, cancellation and payment failure, using the payment test environment. | No double booking or unexplained financial difference is accepted in test cases. Know the share of confirmed appointments that became visits, not only the sign-up count. |
| **Days 46–90** | Compare the messaging channel, the site and the marketplace, and review the objective effects on invoicing, inventory and customer service. Test in Damascus a small category or neighborhood independently of Saudi Arabia. | Expand only if onboarding can be repeated, at least one economic indicator improves without a decline in reliability, and real retention appears across activation cohorts. |

## The required measurement dashboard

- **Operations:** the proposed main measure for the system is **completed visits per active paying branch**, with protection indicators such as double-booking frequency, booking time and message rejection. Define an active branch by real operating activity, not by a mere sign-in.
- **Marketplace:** the main measure is **completed visits from new discovery through the marketplace**; watch the share of searches with available options, choice → confirmation → attendance, return within 30/90 days, and first visit per neighborhood/category. Distinguish electronic payment from a confirmed booking, and GMV from collected value and revenue.
- **Software:** track time to first value, trial-to-paid conversion, 30/90-day retention, churn, CAC, its payback period, and margin after support, messaging and payment cost. Ask for 12 months of aggregated data before using the model in an investment decision.
- **Purchase or partnership decision:** request a migration and export sample covering customers, appointments, invoices and inventory, a renewal fee schedule, the commission policy, documents on encryption, processors and data location, and a demonstration of failure scenarios with your team present. If the decision is an investment, add net subscription revenue by country, paid branches, completed bookings, each channel's contribution, and cancellation and retention rates by cohort.

## Closing conclusion

Toptalla's potential value lies not in the booking button alone but in keeping the appointment, the payment and the customer record consistent across channels. The public evidence is enough to put it on an evaluation list and start a limited trial, but it is not enough to prove leadership, actual financial feasibility, or the suitability of all its plans for Saudi Arabia and Syria. The best decision is built on the result of a measurable trial and a clear agreement, not on the number of features or downloads.
